Side project

Ask Maria

An airfare assistant that lives in Telegram. You tell her where you'd rather be in plain English; she watches the fares and messages back only when there's something worth knowing.

Stack
Python 3.13 + SQLite, Telegram Bot API, Amadeus fares, launchd schedules
Landing page
Static HTML + vanilla JS, no build step
Status
Invite-only beta, running daily

Background

Price alerts tell you a number went down. They don't tell you whether the fare is worth taking, whether the seller is trustworthy, or what the trip actually costs once bags and self-transfers are counted. Maria is the version of that tool that answers those questions instead — in a chat, in the app people already have open.

How it works

  • No commands, no forms — "anywhere sunny in half term, two of us, under £400 each" is a valid instruction. The interface is a conversation because the request is genuinely fuzzy, and a date picker would force a precision the traveller doesn't have yet.
  • Two scheduled jobs, not a server — a launchd agent checks fares twice a day and a second one polls the inbox every minute, so she replies in about a minute without anything running in the foreground.
  • Judgement, not just a number — each alert says whether she'd take the fare and why: seller screened against ATOL, IATA and Trustpilot, one-ways and baggage totalled, cheaper-but-worse options called out.
  • Multi-user by invite — an invite table, per-user watchlists and price history in SQLite, so the beta can grow past one person.

Design decisions worth defending

  • The copy makes no promise the system can't keep — no "24/7", no "instant", no cheapest-price guarantee. She "usually replies within a minute or two", and the footer states plainly that this is an information service, not a travel agent, which is a regulated description in the UK.
  • The two quotes are labelled as mine, not as customers' — the design comp came with invented testimonials attached to invented people. Real results under a real name are worth less as marketing and more as trust.
  • Signup is two steps on purpose — the landing page only carries an email across; the second page asks where you're dreaming of flying, which segments the waitlist from day one and tells me who to admit next.
