Background

Create a white label wallet for users to experience seamless In-Car Payments

A white label wallet on wheels, enabling users to make seamless and secure payments from their automotive operating system. Create wireframes, mock-ups, and prototypes that will serve as the foundation for a coded product and for pitching purposes utilizing the Google Automotive OS.

Scenarios including:

  • 🔹 Device binding
  • 🔹 Wallet management flow
  • 🔹 Function on Demand (FOD)
  • 🔹 Quick service restaurant (QSR)
  • 🔹 EV Charging
  • 🔹 Parking

Key users

We are designing for drivers and passengers. That said, the demo app is for OEM brand partners who will own the customer journey.

Driving habits

  • Daily commute (city & highway)
  • 🔹 Frequent quick errands and weekend trips.
  • 🔹 Heavy use of navigation, music, and hands-free calls.

Needs & Goals

  • 🔹 Seamless payments
  • 🔹 Efficiency
  • 🔹 Convenience
  • 🔹 Integration
  • 🔹 Security
“My car is basically my mobile office and entertainment hub. If I can pay for my coffee, charge up, and even unlock a new feature, all from the touchscreen, that's a game-changer for my busy life.”

Design challenges

With limited front-end developer assistance and restrictions imposed by the Automotive OS design system, we made necessary adjustments to ensure the experience is as realistic as possible, despite the limited content that can be integrated.

Results

The wallet went from a blank page to a demo an OEM partner could sit in front of: six payment scenarios designed white-label on Google Automotive OS, shown on the stand at CES in 2024 and again in 2025. It secured the technical partnerships the programme was set up to win in 2023, and became the artefact the pitch for a first pilot client was built on.

Retrospective

🪚 Trade-off

The Automotive OS design system decides most of what a screen can look like, and we had limited front-end help. So the question stopped being "what is the best interface" and became "what is the most realistic thing we can put in a car and defend to an OEM". I designed to the constraint rather than around it — a demo a partner half-believes is worth less than a plainer one they can ship.

♟️ Prioritisation

Six use cases, one demo. We ranked them by journey length and frequency rather than by how interesting they were to design: a local errand, a daily commute and a road trip carry different payment moments, and the short, frequent ones earn their screen space. Parking and EV charging survived that cut; the more novel ideas did not.

🚦 Who it is really for

The user in the car is a driver, but the buyer is an OEM brand partner who will own the whole customer journey. Designing white-label was the decision that let one spec serve both — every brand can put its own skin on it without the payment logic changing underneath.
