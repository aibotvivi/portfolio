Merchant Onboarding

Activating payments and enabling payouts across web and mobile

Project duration
2025 Q3
Team
Myself, Product lead, Product manager, Full stack devs, Legal, QA

Background

MPS allows merchants to edit multiple applications at the same time under the same email address

This feature is an add-on for merchants to work on a few applications at the same time.

Problem statements

Merchants face inefficiencies and delays during onboarding to multiple programs due to receiving Sign Up links instead of Sign In links and the lack of automatic Sign In flow for ongoing processes.

Problem 1

  • Identification of the problem — Merchants receive Sign Up links instead of Sign In links when invited to additional programs, leading to inefficiencies.
  • Impact — Redundant steps and wasted time.

Problem 2

  • Identification of the problem — Multiple invites do not automatically trigger the Sign In flow for ongoing onboarding processes, causing delays.
  • Impact — Delays and complications in completing additional onboardings.

User requirements

  • As a user of a merchant that has already started onboarding to one program I want to receive an invite for onboarding to another program with Sign In link instead of Sign Up, so that I can save some time while starting the new onboarding
  • As a user of a merchant who has received multiple invites to onboard to different programs while one of those onboardings has been started I want the rest of the invites to start Sign In flow for me, so that I can make the onboardings to other programs using the old invites

User flow

User story and high level flow.

Outcome

The flow detects an existing account at the invitation stage and sends a Sign In link rather than a Sign Up one, and routes every later invitation into the session the merchant already has open. The redundant account-creation steps that had been holding up onboarding to additional programmes come out of the journey entirely.

Retrospective

🪚 Trade-off

The obvious fix was a better sign-up screen. The right fix was to send a different link — the problem was never the form, it was that the invitation had already decided the merchant was a stranger. Moving the decision back to the invitation stage meant changing a backend behaviour rather than a page, which is a harder conversation and a smaller design.

♟️ Designing for the second time, not the first

Onboarding is usually designed for a merchant who has never been here before. This one is for a merchant who is already mid-application on another programme — the same person, a different state. Most of the work was deciding what the product should remember about them, and what it has to ask again for legal reasons even when it already knows the answer.

🚦 Working inside KYB

Nothing in a Know Your Business flow can be removed just because it is slow. Every step I took out had to be one that legal and QA agreed was redundant rather than merely inconvenient, which is why the change lands on routing and session handling and not on the checks themselves.
